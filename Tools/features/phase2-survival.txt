# High Water Mark - phase 2: survival systems and the full journey
# Plan this AFTER phase 1 is built and committed, so task briefs can name real files:
#   .\claude-batch.ps1 -Features Tools\features\phase2-survival.txt -PlanOnly
# The full design is in game-spec.txt at the repo root; each feature names the spec section to read.

1. Flood timeline after the surge (spec SX 03). Drive the flood state from the game clock in phases. Draining,
   days 2 to 5: slopes clear first, valleys run as brown torrents, every river in huge flood, low ground soft silt;
   leaving early is possible but slow and dangerous. Trapped water, weeks: hollows found by the fill pass hold salt
   lakes that fall a few centimetres a day. The Levels stay a shallow brackish inland sea all game. Gas hollows
   (heavier than air, pooled in valleys and towns) clear by day 5 or as wind clears them. Rivers become fordable
   again as they fall, so what is open changes day by day.

2. Food, water and energy (spec SX 05). Track calories, hydration, fatigue, health and morale per person.
   Walking with a pack over rough ground burns 3,500 to 4,500 kcal a day, scaled by load, distance and terrain;
   a child needs less. A tin is 0.4 kg for only 300 to 450 kcal; smoked or dried meat has about three times the
   calories of fresh per kilo. Water: streams above the mark are safe after filtering; below it they are salty and
   dirty. A 230 g gas canister boils about 20 litres and can't be refilled, so wood fires matter later.
   Eating, drinking, filtering, cooking and resting are actions that take game time.

3. Scavenging by band (spec SX 03, "Bands below the line"). Search buildings, cars and wrack piles from the
   ground-detail loot. Above the mark: buildings stand with holed roofs, little food. Strandline: the best
   scavenging, sealed tins survive water, but piles are unstable, slow and risky. Scour zone: towns reduced to
   foundations; buried shops and cellars hold loot only if you dig (slow). Floating wrack on standing water can be
   harvested. Taken items are recorded in the tile's saved changes.

4. Map memory and found OS sheets (spec SX 04). The paper map's quality depends on what the character remembers:
   the route home by car (A30, M5, A39) and the Dartmoor walking area are accurate; places in between are sketchier,
   with fewer lanes and approximate contours. Finding a real OS sheet in a farmhouse or a car is a prize item that
   redraws that sheet's area of the paper map accurately.

5. Extend the world to the full journey (spec SX 02 landscapes, SX 04 likely routes). Run the terrain pipeline
   over the whole region from Dartmoor to Priddy on Mendip (about 260 m), covering the direct route (Okehampton,
   Crediton, Tiverton, Taunton), the high route (Exmoor, Brendons, Quantocks) and the east route (Haldon, Blackdown
   Hills), the Somerset Levels, Glastonbury, the Poldens, Wedmore and Cheddar Gorge. Stream terrain and tiles in
   and out by area so memory stays bounded. Check each landscape reads right against the mark: Quantocks ridge
   (Wills Neck 384 m) above a mark of about 300 m; Cheddar Gorge wholly below it; Priddy in the strandline band.

6. Okehampton Camp and the first weight decision (spec SX 01, days 2 to 4). The camp is half wrecked. Its cracked
   armoury holds an L403A1 rifle (3.5 to 5 kg), some ammunition (12 g a round) and a few 30-round magazines
   (0.5 kg loaded). A store room nearby has a .22 air rifle (about 3 kg) and thousands of pellets (a 500-pellet tin
   is 0.3 kg and lasts most of a game). The player can't comfortably carry both guns and a full food load.
   Let the player leave items in a cache that persists on the map and come back for them.

7. Animals and how they spread (spec SX 06). Starting animal populations per tile, generated once in the world
   build script and committed with the rest of the fixed world: rabbits above the mark,
   pheasants and pigeons; roe deer in the valley woods at the moor's edge; red deer on Exmoor and the Quantocks;
   very rare sheep, cattle, pigs and Dartmoor ponies; nothing in the scour zone at first. Deer are placed in a few
   fixed spots, with signs to find. Regrowth: grass returns first on high ground and rain-washed slopes, later
   on low salty flats; each game day move animals a little towards neighbouring tiles with more new grass. Birds
   move from day 1: gulls and crows on the strandline, pigeons and pheasants above the mark, ducks on new water.
   Rabbits and deer spread down into the valleys over the following weeks.

8. Hunting with the .22 air rifle (spec SX 06). Rabbits, pheasants, ducks, pigeons and small birds. Range about
   30 to 40 m and it needs a head shot, so stalking matters (approach, wind, cover). Nearly silent. Ammunition is
   never the limit; time and skill are. A rabbit, pheasant or duck is one meal for two, roasted whole over a fire,
   and should feel like the best part of the day. Add snares as a second quiet option.

9. The service rifle and noise (spec SX 06). For deer. Limited rounds, so every shot is a decision.
   A shot is heard several kilometres away: it scares off nearby animals. Weight trade-off
   already comes from the pack model.

10. Fishing (spec SX 06). Slow, quiet and steady, suited to camp days. Tackle from wrecked houses and tackle shops,
   or a crude line with safety-pin hooks from the first aid kit. Moorland streams above the mark: small brown trout,
   a few hundred kcal each. Trapped salt lakes: bass and mullet for a few weeks until the lakes shrink or go stale.
   Rivers below the mark: almost dead at first, recovering slowly. The Levels: eels and coarse fish from the raft,
   plus overnight eel traps. Stranded fish in draining pools: free food for a day, then they rot.

11. Spoilage, butchery and smoking (spec SX 06, "The big kill decision"). Fresh meat goes off within one to two days
   in July without cooling. A roe deer gives about 8 to 12 kg of meat, a red deer 40 kg or more. Feasting on fresh
   cuts and offal first restores health and morale. Smoking at camp for one or two days turns about 10 kg of fresh
   meat into 3 to 4 kg that keeps for weeks, but the fire is visible from far off. Bones for stock, fat for cooking,
   hide for a groundsheet or raft lashings, scraps for the dogs. Needs a camp site, water and wood. Score each kill
   on how much was used and show the figure. Butchery fades to black and shows a results screen (meat, hide, waste),
   optionally with a short line from the child.

12. Dogs, up to two (spec SX 07). Staffies that outlived their owners, won over by feeding them and sitting quietly
   across a few encounters. They warn of animals at night, flush pheasants and rabbits, track a wounded
   deer and raise morale. A 20 kg dog needs about 1,000 kcal a day and can carry a dog pack of a few kilos at most.
   Dogs can be injured and need rest or carrying, but cannot die unless an option is switched on.

13. Rafts and the Levels (spec SX 01 finale, SX 03). Build a raft from wrack, hide lashings or found boats.
   Move by raft, wading and between higher ground at the Poldens, Burtle, Glastonbury (the Isle of Avalon again) and
   Wedmore, over a shallow brackish sea with drowned trees, roofs and roads under the surface. Every route ends by
   crossing water here, so the raft is part of the final act.

14. Save and load. Save the world data version, game clock, party state, pack and caches, paper map marks, per-tile player
   changes and animal populations; load restores the same world. The fixed world data itself is never saved, only the changes made to it. Unit-test a save/load round trip.
